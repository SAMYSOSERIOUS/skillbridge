    How this works

    The frontier

    For your occupation, SkillBridge scores a move to every other occupation on three things at once: pay gain (median wage difference), retraining effort (a skill-gap score built from O*NET skill levels, education zones, and how similar the day-to-day tasks actually are), and AI risk change (three independent research indices, combined). The green frontier is the set of moves where you can't do better on one count without giving up another.

    Retraining effort

    The score adds four ingredients: how different the skill profiles are, how much you'd need to level up skills you already have (moving up costs more than moving down), whether the job needs a higher education/preparation level (O*NET Job Zones — a single move can climb at most one), and how different the actual work tasks are, measured on 19,000 real O*NET task statements. Moves also need real national headcount, so wage-outlier niche jobs don't show up.

    AI risk, honestly

    Nobody can measure AI displacement precisely, so SkillBridge never shows one number alone. Three independent sources — an academic ability-based index (AIOE), OpenAI's task-based ratings, and Microsoft Research's score from observed real Copilot usage — are each shown as percentiles, with a flag when they disagree. Disagreement is information, not noise.

    Data & limitations

    Sources

    • O*NET basic skills, Job Zones & 19k task statements (US Dept. of Labor)
    • BLS OEWS national median wages, May 2021 release
    • AIOE index — Felten, Raj & Seamans (2021)
    • "GPTs are GPTs" — OpenAI / Eloundou et al. (MIT license)
    • "Working with AI" — Microsoft Research (CC BY 4.0)

    What this can't tell you

    • O*NET profiles describe the typical job holder, not you — treat moves as estimates of typical feasibility, not personal guarantees.
    • Exposure indices measure exposure, not certain job loss; the sources legitimately disagree for some occupations.
    • Wages are national point-in-time medians — no cost-of-living adjustment; top-coded wages (≥ $208k/yr in this release) are flagged, not guessed.
    • Licensed occupations (pilots, air-traffic control, healthcare) require certifications and hour requirements the model cannot measure.
    • This is analysis of public data, not career advice.

    About

    SkillBridge is an open-source portfolio project: a multi-objective career decision engine, not a job-similarity recommender. Every number on this page comes from the public datasets above through a tested data pipeline (DuckDB + dbt), and every formula and threshold is documented in the repository.

    This product uses the O*NET Database by the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration (USDOL/ETA), used under the CC BY 4.0 license. Wage data: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, OEWS. AI-exposure indices: Felten, Raj & Seamans (AIOE); Eloundou et al. (OpenAI, MIT license); Tomlinson et al. (Microsoft Research, CC BY 4.0).
